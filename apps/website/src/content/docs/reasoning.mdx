Sometimes the most useful thing to save is why you chose an approach.

Magistr's reasoning traces are explanations the agent deliberately records.
They can hold a question, findings from code or research, alternatives,
uncertainty, and a conclusion. They are useful when the next session asks,
“Why did we do it this way?”

## Start with a question you can answer

In the [Bevy game recording](/#workflow), the question is: **does the opponent
need to search ahead?**

One candidate takes an immediate win, blocks an immediate loss, then chooses
the center or a corner. It is short and easy to read. But can it handle a fork,
where the other player creates two winning threats?

That is something we can test.

## Follow the evidence

The experiment enumerated legal human replies. The simple policy had no losing
game when it played first, but lost when it played second.

The recorded losing sequence uses cells numbered left to right, top to bottom:

```text
X: 1    O: 5
X: 8    O: 3
X: 7    O: 9
X: 4    → X wins
```

At X's move in cell 7, O has two threats to deal with. Blocking cell 9 leaves
cell 4 open.

The first experiment also had an incorrect assertion: it expected a losing
game for both marks. That failure remains in the check history. We corrected
the experiment to report its findings.

## Give an investigation its own branch

The opponent question branches from the game plan. Its findings lead back to
a conclusion: use minimax for either mark.

A reasoning branch has an origin, its own steps, and a status. When it reaches
a conclusion, that conclusion can refer to the branch. Abandoned investigations
remain in the history too.

Open **Reasoning** in the walkthrough. Select a step to read its context,
finding, rationale, and next action. The lines show recorded relationships
between those steps, including branch origins and conclusions.

A list of “plan, code, test” would describe the work sequence. It would not
explain this decision.

## Keep the conclusion and its limits

The chosen opponent searches the remaining legal games. The check found:

| Computer plays | Wins | Draws | Losses |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| X | 82 | 10 | 0 |
| O | 368 | 153 | 0 |

These counts cover every legal human reply to this deterministic opponent.
They are not a count of every possible tic-tac-toe game.

The recorded conclusion keeps the evidence and the reason for choosing full
search. A later session can find it before changing the opponent.

## Revise an answer when new evidence arrives

Record a revision and its reason when an assumption stops holding. Relations
between steps can show support, contradiction, or dependence. Update affected
roadmap work as well.

A **pin** keeps an important conclusion easy to retrieve. A reasoning
**checkpoint** helps review open questions, branches, and decisions affected
by revisions. It is a review of the reasoning record, not a source backup.

## Know what is being saved

Magistr saves what the agent records through its tools. It does not automatically
copy every chat message or expose a model's private internal thinking.

Ask for a useful explanation with evidence. Link it to the work it informs.
[Checking the work](/docs/verification) explains how a saved explanation differs
from a command that actually ran.
