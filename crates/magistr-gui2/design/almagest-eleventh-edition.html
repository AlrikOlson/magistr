Terminal interaction

Interactive terminal examples show streaming output, scroll-follow controls, press-and-hold termination, and dock resizing. Reduced motion renders the settled state.

ChangeThe call
Dock and terminationThe dock, maximized on demand (⌘⇧↵), never a separate place. Kill is press-and-hold: hold k or the cap for half a second, the head fills red as you hold, let go early and nothing happens. No second press, no dialog.
time is a railA hairline down the pane’s left edge is the clock. A tick where each new second begins; a dotted break where the program was silent for more than a second; a small stamp only where it earns one — after a silence, or every ten seconds. Ruled paper by day, faint light by night, in the pane’s own dim ink. Always there, never a column of numbers.
the mockups runTry them. Scroll a pane up and the pill appears with what arrived; press it or f to return. Hold the Kill cap. In 16, Lower, Maximize and the card’s Dock move the dock with the 220 ms rise. The run ends, freezes on its exit, and replays.
one rule for both sizesThe card’s eight rows behave exactly like the dock — follow, stop, pill, return. Interactive examples appear in II.k′ and 05.

Terminal presentation

ChangeThe call
Input and sizeRead-only. Nothing pressed reaches the process; Kill is the one verb that touches it. Eight rows in the card, scrolling inside; the dock for more.
a run is a blockThe card is the block. Command · output · exit code · duration as one selectable unit, the way Warp groups a terminal (precedent 1). Its head stays pinned while the body scrolls — VS Code’s sticky command (precedent 2) — and a hairline guide beside the output says what belongs to what.
following the tailNo Follow chip. The pane follows while it is at the bottom; scrolling up stops it, silently; a pill floats at the pane’s foot — ↓ Latest · +42 — counting what arrived, and one press or f returns and resumes. The pattern Slack, shadcn’s message scroller and NN/g describe (precedent 3), and the one GitHub Actions is asked for because its viewer snaps to the bottom (precedent 4).
pop out is a dockThe run rises into a dock over the session, not into another place. Zed and VS Code dock the terminal beside the work (precedent 5); the agent desktops keep live output next to the conversation (precedent 6). The dock rises from the composer’s edge to a dragged height, the feed and the rail stay, the composer stays; ⌘⇧↵ maximizes, esc lowers it. Nothing navigates; the crumb holds.
the program’s own signalsOSC 9;4 progress is a strip under the head; OSC 8 links and file:line are links. Claude Code already emits both (precedent 7); cargo’s directory.rs:122:13 opens in the Changes reading. When a shell reports OSC 133 zones (Ghostty, WezTerm, iTerm2), the dock walks commands with ⌘↑/⌘↓ (precedent 8).
time in the gutterHover shows when each row arrived. A faint gutter of elapsed stamps, drawn only while the pointer is over the pane — the block’s duration expanded to its rows.
Kill is deliberateKill asks once, inline. The head reads Kill cargo nextest? k again · esc for three seconds; a second k kills; the receipt says it was you.
renderingGPU cells, bounded scrollback, no jank. The pane is the window’s own cell grid (wgpu); 10,000 rows of scrollback; the newest row arrives once; scrolling keeps momentum. Warp and Ghostty set the bar (precedents 1, 9); the cockpit already renders this way.

Live command output

ChangeThe call
Card behaviorA card opens in place. The ledger head’s verbs are always drawn; a checkpoint row shows Preview always, Restore · Fork on hover or focus. The identity band is one row of essentials — the clock stays in the header chip, the tokens with their headroom stay in the foot. A Check card opens into the gate’s own diagnostic rows.
a run is a terminalAnything magistr shells out runs on a pseudo-terminal and streams. The agent’s terminal requests, the gates the loop runs and the gates the MCP tool runs all spawn under a pty; every byte reaches the cockpit as it is written. The agent still reads plain text.
the pane is the program’sHue is data here. The pane draws the theme’s ANSI-16 — the sky’s at night, the chart’s by day — and passes truecolor through the contrast floor, so a dim grey stays legible and a red stays the program’s red. The one place §3’s “state codes only” yields, on purpose.
the card’s body is the live paneA running Run, Check or MCP card opens into its terminal’s tail. It follows the tail until you scroll up, breathes while it runs, blinks one cursor, and freezes with its exit code in the receipt line when the run ends.
pop out (16)The terminal full height at altitude 3. Pop out from the card; Kill · Copy · Follow in the header; the foot counts lines and time; Esc climbs to the session and the pane keeps running behind you.
read-onlyYou watch; you do not type. Nothing you press reaches the agent’s shell — a keystroke into a process the agent is reasoning about is a hazard, not a feature. Kill is the one verb that touches the process.
the honest edgeThe Claude provider’s own Bash is not live. Claude Code runs it and reports a finished result; its card fills when the result lands, and says so.

The session, whole

A card’s receipt strip, a search hit or a checkpoint row’s count opens the receipt at altitude 3: the transcript, the checkpoint it sits inside, the rollback, the files it touched, the think and ship refs, and its jumps.
continuitySix hops, all tested. Sky card ⇆ session; ⌘K session rows resume into a bound session; the chapter’s recording and trace refs land on the session or the step; a promoted signal jumps to its session; the ship moment leaves the session for the sky.

Settings

ChangeThe call
settings layoutSidebar of scoped groups, not tabs. Use a searchable sidebar (This app · the project under its sigil · Team, read-only groups labeled at the nav level) and one pane per group, ending with its source file.
scope labelsScopes are named, never inferred. The project section says “This project” and shows an identity row (sigil · name · path); the pane header repeats the scope. A bare project name is ambiguous — fatally so when the project shares the app’s name.
controls match contentA theme is a visual choice, so its picker shows the media — three live preview cards (sky / chart / follow OS), selected by ring-and-check. Booleans get real switches. Segmented pills survive only for genuinely enumerable text choices.

indiscriminate glassmorphismBlur belongs only to overlays (palette, plan card, search, the new-project modal); in-flow cards are opaque.

timid even paletteStructure is neutral + one accent (mauve = live/attention); green, amber, red, blue, teal are state codes and workstream hues, never decoration.

Component conventions

NormalizedThe standard
moneyLabels: 2.07 USD. Meters: unitless pairs (1.84 / 12.00). The $ glyph does not appear in the UI.
timeClocks 04:12 · durations 12 min, 4 h, 2 d. One format per unit.
statesOne vocabulary: Ready · Executing · Review · Stuck (needs a decision) · Blocked (cannot start) · Done · Idle. Every chip, card and star uses these words and no others.
one lit exitAudited: exactly one filled button per screen, everywhere (the bad day and the signals inbox each had two; fixed).
no naked textFinal sweep: the survey’s file-written lines were the last naked strings; they are receipt rows now.
iconsCoverage completed: search field, palette rows, deliverables caption, survey receipts, the created-project toast’s sigil.
namesControls name chunk ids (Claim harmonic-engine); lists may show display names.

The life layers

DecisionThe call
iconographyA custom celestial-technical set. 16 px grid, 1.5 px stroke, and one signature: stroke terminals end in a star-dot. Icons mark kinds, navigation and empty states; buttons stay text verbs; icon-only buttons only for utilities.
sigilsA generated mark per project — a mini-constellation derived from the name — in the galaxy, the breadcrumb, and reports.
data vizRing gauges, an activity heatmap, and bars replace printed numbers where a shape reads faster: roadmap completion is a ring, the month is a heatmap.
materialGrain on the night sky, glass on the floats, parallax on pan. Day stays clean paper — the texture belongs to the light medium.
waitingLoading is designed: skeleton rows and ghost stars, on the specimen sheet like everything else.
typeBoth moves: a Berkeley-class mono engraves every numeral and id; the serif display face is reserved for report titles and covers. UI text stays system sans.

Two laws

LawThe rule
no naked textEvery string in the UI belongs to a component. Status rows, receipt rows, meters with threshold ticks, stat blocks, key-hints, mode badges, gate LEDs. A free-floating prose line is a missing component. The components are the murmur is status rows, spend is a meter, receipts are rows, hints are key caps, charter rules wear mode badges.
copy is STEIn-app copy follows Simplified Technical English. Short sentences. Imperative verbs on actions. Noun labels on data. Numerals. No metaphor in any string — the constellation is a rendering, the words say Roadmap, Projects, Report. Personality is delivered by design and motion only, never by copy.

II · The components — specimens

Every element the fourteen views are assembled from, rendered once, canonically. If a view draws something not on this sheet, either the sheet grows or the view is wrong.

II.a · controls

The copy rule. Controls name real objects — roadmap, chunk, project, session — never the metaphor, because the metaphor changes medium with the theme (“Accept this sky” read absurd on the day chart). The ambient voice may keep the poetry, but only in words true of both media: one charts by day and by night; a button never asks you to accept weather.

V · The nineteen views

Birth to chapter to the book’s own machinery, in order, every one built from the specimens above.

F1 · Choose the ground

“＋ New project” pressed on the galaxy. One dashed slot — the control language's only legal use of a dashed outline — and two raised alternatives. The galaxy waits underneath.

F2 · The survey

magistr reads the ground — magistr init's real autodetect, drawn as a survey sweep. Facts land on the left as they are found; the scaffold writes itself on the right as receipts. Nothing here is decorative: every row is a real detection or a real file written.

F3 · The interview

Three questions, not thirty — the card language from the brief, reused. What should this become, what must never break, and how free is the agent to move. The answers seed the roadmap's goal, the gates, and the charter's starting position.

F4 · The charting

The agent drafts the first constellation from the survey and your answers — and you watch it drawn: stars placed one by one, dependency edges pulled between them, workstreams already wearing their hues. It is a proposal, not a fact: every star is editable before you accept.

F5 · First light

The sky accepted: eight dashed stars waiting, none lit — and the first ready chunk already glowing at the edge of the room. A new project's empty sky is not an empty state; it is a map of everything to come.

01 · Galaxy — every project

The top of the world. Every project as a cluster: its shipped history is its density, its live sessions breathe mauve, a question anywhere glows amber. This screen answers “switch projects” by making projects places.

02 · First open — the brief

You've been away six hours. The sky waits underneath, dimmed; the story lands as cards, worst first, each one divable. Dismiss the stack and you're home.

03 · Sky, at rest — your hands off

Leave the window open while the fleet works. After a few seconds of no input, the chrome exhales: no bars, no buttons, no numbers — the constellation, one murmur line, nothing else. Touch the mouse and it all fades back in 150 ms.

04 · Sky, awake — chrome faded back in

One project, one screen: the constellation is the roadmap, the sessions are its moving lights, and the next chunk is one press away. This is home, and it is also the idle screen — a month of work makes it visibly denser.

05 · Session — the theater, and a run peered into

Down one altitude: the theater. The cap says what the session is; prose streams with the emissive sweep; a card opens where it is into the detail its verb deserves; the rail is bound to what really governs the session and is where the loop is steered from. One glance-stable frame.

06 · Moment — a plan asking

The deepest altitude: one decision, everything needed to make it, nothing else. Two-phase — the plan before, a receipt after — with the checkpoint named so “allow” is never a leap.

07 · Moment — a review

The other moment: a finished session asking for your eye. Diff-first, gates in the header, framed as “what changed since the last verified state” — because that is the actual question.

08 · The ship moment — in the workstream's own hue

The flash identifies the completed workstream. Desktop GUI work ignites mauve, settling to the universal shipped-green; engine work would flash blue, theme work teal. Over months the sky becomes a color-coded history of where the effort went.

09 · A shipped star — the chapter and its deliverables

Click any lit star and the almagest opens to its page: the serif chapter on the left (the same document the share page publishes), the deliverables shelf on the right — stills, filmstrips, the recording, the receipts. Everything a stakeholder would want, collected while the work happened.

15 · Receipt — one tool call as a record

Altitude 3, the record kind. A card names its receipt; opening the receipt shows the whole of one tool call and everything it is tied to: the checkpoint it sits inside, the rollback that names it, the files it touched, the reasoning step and the ship action it belongs to. Search hits and checkpoint rows open here too.

16 · The run dock — a run, risen over the session

Not another place: the running card’s terminal rises from the composer’s edge into a dock, the way Zed and VS Code dock a terminal beside the work. The feed stays above it, the rail stays beside it, the composer stays under it, the crumb never moves. Drag the grip for height; ⌘⇧↵ takes the whole stage; esc lowers it and the run keeps going.

10 · Settings — a sidebar of scopes, a pane per group

⌘, from anywhere. Scopes and their groups live in a searchable sidebar; the pane shows one group with its source named. Controls match their content: a theme is a visual choice, so its picker is visual; booleans are switches; team-owned values say who owns them.

11 · The reasoning trace — its own moment view

The reasoning trace is a full view. Entered from the session (or a chapter's provenance chip), the think-step feed at reading width — pins held, branches visible, every step's execution refs jumping to the receipts they produced.

12 · Signals — the inbox at sky altitude

What stakeholders asked for, waiting to be researched and promoted. A promoted signal becomes a star; the inbox is where the sky listens.

13 · Search — finding, not jumping

⌘⇧F over any altitude: full text across the whole book — chapters, receipts, trace steps, sessions. Results say what they are and when they happened; enter opens the thing at the match.

14 · A bad day — the doctrine, demonstrated

The agent binary is gone, the budget breaker tripped an hour ago, and an attach feed died. No error screens: the same cards, worst first, each with the one verb that fixes it — and the sky tells the same story.
